The parent guide

You're in charge. Here's the manual.

11+ Test Coach gives your child a friendly, self-contained place to practise — and gives you a PIN-protected Parent Mode with full control over how they practise, how much help they get, and what they're working towards. This page walks through all of it, with real screenshots.

Step one

Getting set up

Setup takes a few minutes and the app does the thinking for you:

  1. Add your child — name, age, and a profile they'll recognise. You can add more than one child and switch between them anywhere in Parent Mode.
  2. Pick your target schools from the built-in database of 248 grammar and selective schools. The app automatically limits practice to the subjects those schools actually test — no wasted effort.
  3. Choose your 4-digit PIN. Everything parent-facing sits behind it: settings, progress, rewards, data. The child side stays simple and safe.
  4. Set up PIN recovery. Email yourself a one-time recovery code (Tools → Support → PIN recovery). Forget the PIN later? After five wrong attempts the app offers a reset that accepts that code — how it works.

From then on, opening the app lands on a friendly profile picker: your child taps their name and goes straight to practice; you tap Parent Mode and enter your PIN.

Step two

The starting assessment

Before the daily missions begin, each subject offers a starting assessment: 20 questions drawn from every topic in that subject, at a mix of difficulties, so the app can work out where your child actually is. It is deliberately not a test — there is no timer, no pass mark, and Taco stays quiet so the picture is honest.

It matters because everything afterwards is built on it. The result sets the difficulty your child's practice starts at, so the first week is neither demoralising nor pointless. It takes about 15 minutes per subject.

You control whether it happens: switch on Taco invites your child in Parent Mode → Starting Assessments and Taco does the asking. Until a subject's assessment is done, every route into that subject's practice leads back to it — so it can't be skipped by accident.

What your child sees

Twenty questions, one at a time, with a progress bar and no clock. They can go back and change an answer. Reading questions come with their passage attached, and maths and verbal reasoning keep the squared-paper notepad for working out.

They can stop at any point. Tap Quit and the app offers Save and Exit — their place and every answer so far are kept, and the assessment is waiting exactly where they left it next time they open the app. Even if the phone is closed mid-question, nothing is lost. Discard is there for an assessment started by mistake.

Nothing about it is scored in front of them. The screen at the end talks about where to start, not what they got wrong.

What it tells you

The finish screen is deliberately gentle with your child and genuinely useful to you:

  • A suggested starting level — Building blocks, Making progress or Ready to stretch — which the app immediately adopts for that subject's practice.
  • Looking comfortable — the topics they handled well.
  • Good skills to practise next — the topics to spend time on, with the score behind each one.

The same detail lands in Parent Mode. The assessment is recorded exactly like a completed mission, so its 20 questions flow into your progress charts, subject accuracy, weekly summary and PDF reports, and every question they got wrong is waiting in Mistakes to practise again. Your child earns XP for it too — effort counts, whatever the score.

Assessments can be retaken after a fortnight if you want a fresh read, from Parent Mode → Starting Assessments.

What your child sees

A little every day, guided by Taco

Each subject gives your child a daily mission — around ten questions, mixed across the topics that need attention, sized to take about eight minutes. Finishing earns XP, keeps their streak alive and unlocks treats for Taco, the app's endlessly encouraging hamster coach.

  • Quick Quiz for a fast burst on any topic.
  • Beat the Clock mode to build exam pace, gently — running out of time never blocks an answer.
  • Mock tests in the real format when you decide they're ready.

During any set of questions, Taco walks along the progress bar at the top — a step forward with every answer, with the bar catching him up at the finish. It's a small thing that gives children a reason to see a mission through (there's more on it in the kids' guide).

Every question has a clear explanation afterwards, and children can report any question that seems off — reports arrive in your Parent Mode.

Right on the home screen

Taco moves in next to the games

The Today's Practice widget puts your child's streak, today's mission progress and XP right on the home screen — with Taco waving from the corner. It sits exactly where children look most, so today's mission is staring back at them before they can open anything else. The streak flame they've built and the questions still to go are a nudge no reminder notification can match.

It updates by itself as they practise — the progress bar fills, the XP climbs — and at midnight it resets for the new day while the streak carries on. If you have more than one child, it follows whoever practised most recently.

To add it (takes ten seconds, on the child's device):

  1. Press and hold an empty spot on the home screen until the apps jiggle.
  2. Tap Edit in the top corner, then Add Widget.
  3. Search for 11+ Test Coach and choose a size — small tucks Taco in the corner, medium gives him room to wave properly.
  4. Tap Add Widget, then Done.

Your call

What kind of coach do you want Taco to be?

Families disagree about hints — some want a tutor at their child's elbow, some want exam conditions. So the help level is your setting, not ours. In Parent Mode → Manage Child → Taco Help settings you choose how much help Taco offers, per child:

Help availability, read-aloud and sounds — all per child.

The encourager

All Taco help

Two escalating clues, a worked example and read-aloud on every practice question. Great for building confidence early in the journey.

The balancer

One clue only

A single nudge per question, then your child is on their own. The independent-answer XP bonus quietly rewards not asking at all.

The exam-ready

No Taco help

Practice under test conditions. And whatever you choose here, mock tests always run with help off — scores are always honest.

Read aloud

Why Taco sounds the way he does

Taco can read questions, hints, explanations and reading passages aloud. He does it with your device's own voice, not an internet service — and that is a deliberate choice.

Apps with impressively lifelike AI voices work by sending the text to a company's servers every time they speak. For us that would mean your child's questions, answers and mistakes leaving your phone. We won't do that, so Taco speaks entirely on the device.

The honest trade-off: iPhones and iPads only pre-install a basic voice, which sounds flat and robotic. Apple's far better Premium and Enhanced voices are free — they just need downloading once:

  1. Open the iOS Settings app (not the 11+ Test Coach app).
  2. Go to Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices → English.
  3. Tap the download arrow next to a British voice marked Premium (or Enhanced).
  4. Back in 11+ Test Coach: Parent Mode → Manage Child → Taco Help → Taco's voice, and choose it.

That download comes from Apple, not from us, and only happens once — afterwards read-aloud works completely offline, on a plane or in the car. On the same screen you can hear every installed voice, and nudge Taco's pitch and speed until he sounds right for your child.

Motivation that means something

Rewards, step by step

XP and streaks keep practice fun inside the app. Rewards connect that effort to the real world — you decide the treat, you decide the price:

  1. Open Tools → Rewards and tap +.
  2. Name the reward — a cinema trip, a new book, a day out, a bike. Anything your family cares about.
  3. Pick the XP it costs (presets from 100 to 2,000, or type your own) and tap Add reward.

Your child sees a progress bar filling towards something real. You can also switch on Goal rewards — a treat for hitting an XP target within a week or a month. There's deliberately no failure state: an unmet goal simply rolls forward, so rewards stay motivating and never punishing.

The currency behind it all

How XP works — fair by design.

XP drives everything your child unlocks: Taco's outfits, weekly goals and the real-world rewards you set. So the rules are built to be fair — effort always earns something, but getting questions right earns far more, and gaming the system doesn't work.

✏️

Practice questions

1 XP for answering a question, right or wrong — effort is never worthless. A correct answer adds +6, +8 or +11 depending on difficulty, and +2 more if it was solved without Taco's help. So a child who gets 6 of 7 right earns several times more than one who rushes through guessing.

🔁

Retries that mean something

New questions pay in full. Retry a question you got wrong today and beat it — the full correct bonus is yours, because fixing a mistake is exactly the learning we want. Re-answering something already right today pays nothing, so repeating easy wins can't farm XP.

🏅

Session bonuses

A perfect session of 5+ questions earns +10. And a session that would otherwise show zero (all repeats, nothing new beaten) still shows a small "kept practising" award — honest work never reads as worthless.

⏱️

Mock tests

Sitting a mock pays 1 XP per question of its length — a 50-question paper is worth more than a 20 before accuracy even counts. Each correct answer adds +4, and accuracy milestones pay the same at any length: 50% +15 · 70% +25 · 85% +40. Only the first two mocks per subject each day earn XP.

📅

Showing up

Finishing a daily plan earns +25, an on-time assignment +30, and weekly goals (questions, practice days, mocks) +30 each. Consistency compounds — a child who practises a little every day steadily out-earns a weekend crammer.

🔍

Fully transparent

Every results screen shows the exact breakdown — questions answered, correct answers, bonuses — so your child always knows why they earned what they earned. Same performance, same XP, every time.

The long game

Mock tests and real readiness

When you're ready, schedule real-format, timed mock tests in any subject — 20 to 50 questions, no hints, honest scores. The Mocks tab tracks best and average scores per subject and keeps every attempt one tap from a full per-question report.

The Readiness overview is deliberately careful: each subject only unlocks a reliable score after 50 questions, the mock trend appears after three mocks, and consistency tracks practice days — so what you see is evidence, not guesswork.

Where the learning happens

Mistakes are the curriculum

After every quiz, the review screen shows each question with your child's answer beside the correct one — for visual subjects, as pictures — plus a plain-English explanation, the key skill, and the common trap they fell into. Taco can read explanations aloud.

Behind the scenes, every mistake is saved. The child side offers a wrong-first practice session per subject that rebuilds confidence on exactly the questions they missed, then similar ones, then their weakest topics. On your side, Tools → Mistakes organises everything by subject and topic so you can see patterns at a glance.

Reports to share. Data that stays yours.

Export Report creates a clean PDF of progress you can share with a tutor, a partner or a school. Data & Backup lets you export or restore everything the app knows.
