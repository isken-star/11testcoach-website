# Homepage review — 24 October 2026

Reviewed live https://11testcoach.co.uk/ at 375px (iPhone) and 1280px, as a parent
arriving from an Instagram or Apple Search ad. Full-page captures are in
`review/before-phone.png` and `review/before-desktop.png`.

Numbers at 375px: page is **14,714px** long (18 phone screens). First App Store
badge at **377px**, first screenshot starts at **568px**, so on an 812px-tall phone
you get the headline, the badge and the top third of a phone mock-up.

## First screen, first five seconds

- **The headline sells the absence of worksheets, not an outcome.** "Get your child
  ready for the 11+ — without the worksheet pile" is decent, but it says what the
  app replaces, not what it does. Nothing on screen one says the app *teaches*.
  That is now the most important thing about it, and every competitor is a question bank.
- **The offer is out of date.** The hero, the sticky bar ("7 days free, then
  £16.99"), the mid-page box and the pricing headline all lead with a 7-day trial
  that starts a subscription. The app now opens fully for 24 hours with no card and
  nothing to cancel, which is a much easier yes for a wary parent. Right now it isn't mentioned anywhere.
- **Too much small print around the button.** Badge, star line, a trial line,
  then a four-item tick list, all in grey 14–15px text. A tired parent sees a
  wall of reassurance and no clear "do this".
- **The phone in the hero shows the old app**, with four floating stickers
  ("5-day streak", "+120 XP", "Weak area beaten") stacked over it. On a phone
  they cover the screenshot and read as clutter, not as a product.

## First scroll

- A green "October update" strip with an exact count ("All 12,432 questions
  audited") comes next. It's insider news, it is already dated, and the exact count goes stale
  every time the bank changes.
- Then three stat tiles ("248 schools", now wrong, since the list is 250), then four big
  coloured subject tiles that say little ("Codes, logic & word puzzles"). That makes
  three rows of chrome before the parent learns how a day in the app actually works.
- "See the app" shows **old-design screenshots** (Master a Skill, Progress, and a
  Parent Overview from before 1.8). Everything in them has been redesigned.

## Where they drop

- **After the stats and subject tiles (~1,600px).** Nothing so far has answered "what
  will my child actually do each evening, and will it help?". The page shows
  features before it tells a story.
- **The features grid (six cards, ~2,400px on a phone)** is the longest stretch and
  the least persuasive: school-aware practice, assessment mechanics, rewards. It's
  all true, but it reads like a spec sheet. Parents skim it and leave.
- **Pricing is three blocks and ~2,300px.** The 7-day trial card, then the pass,
  then the family plan, each with four bullets. It's honest, but it's heavy, and the first
  thing it tells you is a monthly price.

## What's stale against the new app

- No mention of **Learn it**: 85 short lessons, worked examples with pictures, next-day
  revisits. This is the headline change and it's missing.
- No mention of the **free 24 hours**. The 7-day trial is presented as the way in.
- **248 schools** appears three times; it's 250 now.
- **"12,432"** exact figure in the update strip.
- All screenshots are the old design (home, Master a Skill, Progress, Parent Mode).
- Parent Mode copy describes the old dashboard. The new one opens with "How is
  Casey doing?", strongest and weakest areas, one-tap practice and lesson outcomes.
- Setup is now five short steps and includes a try-it question. The site doesn't say how
  quick it is to start.

## What's working (keep it)

- Real App Store reviews quoted verbatim, and the founder's card about Taira
  passing. They're genuine and they land, but they're buried below the first
  CTA block and look small.
- The privacy promise (no account, no ads, no tracking) is a real differentiator
  for parents. It deserves a short, confident line near the top rather than a big navy
  panel two-thirds of the way down.
- The FAQ answers are honest and specific. Trim the list, keep the answers.
- The sticky bar on phones is the right idea, but it carries the wrong offer.

## Gaps I can't fix with copy

- **Social proof is thin**: three one-line reviews ("Great app!"). Longer
  parent reviews that mention a specific child, subject or result would do more than
  any redesign. The founder needs to collect them; I have not written any.
- No rating count beside the stars. The App Store shows 5.0 from a handful of
  ratings, and that's honest to show, but it's not yet a crowd.

## Plan

One story, top to bottom: **teaches, then practises, then shows you**. The hero says
it and shows the new Home. The primary action is "Download — free for 24 hours, no card".
A three-step "how an evening works" (lesson, practice with Taco's hints, Parent
Mode) uses real new screenshots. The four subjects go in one compact row with lesson counts.
Parent Mode gets its own section. Pricing is simple, with the free day first. Then reviews,
the founder card, a shorter FAQ, and one closing CTA. The features grid, stats row,
update strip and floating stickers are cut.
