How it works

Ten minutes after school, in three steps.

Most 11+ apps are a pile of questions. This one teaches the idea first, so practice builds on something they understand.

  1. 1

    Learn it

    A five-minute lesson explains the topic with pictures and a worked example. Then your child tries it with help and finishes with a quick check. Tomorrow it comes back as a short revisit so it sticks.

  2. 2

    Practise it

    Exam-style questions on the same topic, matched to their level. Stuck? Taco gives a hint, never the answer. Every question has a full explanation, and wrong answers come back until they're beaten.

  3. 3

    You see it

    Parent Mode opens with how they're doing, their strongest and weakest areas, and which lessons they've finished. If a topic needs more work, one tap sets the practice.

Our story

Built at home, for our daughter Taira.

That's us — my wife, me, and our daughter Taira.

Halfway through Year 5 we found out about the 11+ — and that the exam comes round just weeks into Year 6. What we found to help her prepare was either expensive, stuck on a desktop computer, or a pile of papers we'd have to work through ourselves.

Read the rest

So I built what we needed. Over the past six months I've learned the exams inside out, written and checked every question, and made 11+ Test Coach — first for Taira, then for her friends and their classmates. They've tried every screen and told me, bluntly the way children do, what worked and what didn't.

Two things mattered most. Practice had to be fun, because a child who enjoys it will do a little every day — and a little every day is what actually works. And Parent Mode, which I'll admit I built for myself: I wanted to open the app and see at a glance how she's doing in each subject, and exactly which weak areas we should work on together.

Taira sat her 11+ this September, practising on the app right up to exam day — and in October we found out she passed. Since then I've gone back through every question to make the app harder and fairer for the families starting their run-up to next year's exam. I hope it helps your family too.

Common questions

Quick answers.

How does the free 24 hours work?

Download the app and go through the short setup: your child's name and school year, their target schools if you know them, one real question to try with Taco's hint, and a Parent PIN. Then tap Start the free 24 hours. The whole app opens for a day — every subject, every lesson, mock tests and Parent Mode — with no card and nothing to cancel. After that you choose a plan to carry on. The free day is once per device.

How much does it cost after that?

The Exam Year Pass is £99.99 as a single payment for 12 months. It does not auto-renew, so there is nothing to cancel. Monthly is £16.99 a month and starts with a 7-day free trial. Family covers every child in the house: £149.99 for 12 months, or £24.99 a month with a 7-day free trial. See pricing.

What are the lessons, and do they replace practice?

There are 85 short lessons: 30 Maths, 22 English, 15 Verbal Reasoning and 18 Non-Verbal Reasoning. Each one explains a topic with pictures and a worked example, lets your child try it with help, and ends with a quick check. They don't replace practice, they come before it: you'll find them on Home, before a mission, inside Master a Skill and in the lesson library. A finished lesson comes back the next day as a short revisit.

Which schools and regions are covered?

250 grammar and selective schools: grammar schools across England (including Kent), partially selective schools, and Northern Ireland's transfer test schools. Each entry knows which subjects that school tests, so practice stays focused on what your child will actually sit.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone and iPad. The app is designed for both, including iPad layouts in portrait and landscape.

Does my child need an account or an internet connection?

No accounts at all, and once installed the app works fully offline. All data stays on the device — see our privacy policy.

Won't Taco just tell my child the answer?

No. Hints point at the method, never the solution, and every hint is checked automatically against its question's correct answer before release — anything that gives the game away is rejected. Taco is also completely silent during timed mock tests, and answering without opening him earns bonus XP. If you'd still rather limit him, Parent Mode offers one clue only or no help at all per child.

We have two children with an iPad each — does one purchase cover both?

Yes, with the Family plan. It's enabled for Apple Family Sharing, so one purchase unlocks the app on each child's own iPad, under their own Apple ID, for up to six people in your Apple family group. On the second iPad, install the app and tap Restore purchases if it doesn't unlock straight away. (If both iPads are signed into the same Apple ID, any plan works — Family Sharing is only needed when children have their own.)

One thing to know: because we don't upload anything, each child's progress lives on their own iPad. Parent Mode on one device shows that device's child, so you'd check each child on their iPad.

What happens when the Exam Year Pass runs out?

It stops, and that's it — no charge, no renewal, no email chasing you. You'll see a note in the app if you want to buy another year, and your child's progress stays exactly where it is on the device whether you do or not.

How do I cancel, and can I get a refund?

Subscriptions are managed entirely by the App Store: cancel any time in Settings → your Apple ID → Subscriptions, and you keep access until the end of the paid period. If you cancel a monthly plan during its 7-day free trial, you pay nothing. Refunds are handled by Apple at reportaproblem.apple.com.

Where does my child's data go?

Nowhere. Profiles, progress, mistakes and rewards live only on your device — no accounts, no ads, no tracking, no data sold, ever. Payment is handled by the App Store, so we never see payment details either. Full details in the privacy policy.
