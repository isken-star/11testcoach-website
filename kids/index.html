The kids' guide

The side of the app your child will love.

Serious practice doesn't have to feel serious. This is what 11+ Test Coach looks like from your child's seat: missions, XP, a hamster coach called Taco, a floating notepad for working things out, and an app they get to make their own. Show them this page — it's theirs too.

Every day

A mission, not a mountain

Each subject greets your child with today's mission: about ten questions, mixed across the topics that need work, sized for roughly eight minutes. It's deliberately small — the app is built around a-little-every-day, because that's what actually moves 11+ scores.

  • Today's goal on the home screen shows which subjects are done.
  • Quick Practice for spare five-minutes moments.
  • Beat the Clock adds a gentle pace timer — running out of time never blocks an answer, it just shows they're over pace.

Practice reminders (if you turn them on in Parent Mode) arrive as friendly nudges at times you choose — and Holiday Mode pauses everything, streak protected, while you're away.

Ten questions, about eight minutes, +50 XP.
Every question

Race Taco to the finish line

Start any set of questions and Taco is waiting at the beginning of the progress bar. Answer one, and he trots forward — legs going properly — with the orange bar rolling along right behind him. Answer another, and off he goes again.

  • He waits while you think. Taco stands still until an answer goes in, so nothing wriggles about while your child reads the question.
  • The bar chases him down. Taco stays just ahead of the orange, and it catches him up at the very end — finish the mission and they cross the line together.
  • It happens everywhere. Maths, English, verbal, non-verbal, mistakes practice and the starting assessment all get the same little race.

It sounds like a small thing. It isn't: "how far has Taco got?" turns ten questions into something worth finishing, and children can see at a glance that the end is close.

The engine

XP, streaks and things worth unlocking

Every question earns XP for effort — and right answers earn much more, with harder questions worth the most and a quiet bonus for answering without help. Beating a question you got wrong earlier pays in full. Practising on consecutive days builds a streak, and XP totals unlock real things inside the app:

  • New looks for Taco — Thinker, Scholar, Champion and Superstar Taco, each unlocked by lifetime XP.
  • Progress towards rewards you set — the cinema trip or new bike you've promised, tracked on a bar they can see fill up.
  • Weekly goals — practice days this week, questions answered, mocks taken.

There are no coins to buy and nothing to purchase inside the child's app — XP is earned with effort, full stop.

Parents' favourite

The floating working-out notepad

Real working out needs rough paper. In any maths or verbal reasoning quiz or mock, a little Taco bubble floats at the edge of the screen — drag it anywhere, tap it, and a squared-paper notepad opens right on top of the question. Perfect for long multiplication, or for writing out the alphabet when you're cracking a letter code.

  • Draw with a finger — pen, rubber, undo and clear.
  • A fresh page every question — the pad wipes itself when the quiz moves on, so the last sum never clutters the next one.
  • It remembers its place — wherever your child parks the pad, it reappears there next time, even on iPad.

No more hunting for scrap paper at the kitchen table — the working out happens where the question is.

A coach, not an answer machine

Taco's help, on your terms

Stuck on a question? If you've allowed help in Parent Mode, Taco offers a clue, then a stronger hint, then a worked example of a similar question — never just the answer.

You choose the level per child: all help, one clue only, or none at all. Children earn a small XP bonus for answering without asking — and mock tests always run with help off, so scores stay honest. Whatever happens, Taco never makes a child feel bad about a wrong answer.

Their space

Taco's Corner

The Taco tab is your child's own corner of the app — a friendly hub with their day streak and XP today, a shortcut to practise a weak area, and Taco's tip of the day (good habits, sneakily delivered — "before mock tests, get a good night's sleep").

It's also where the fun lives: dressing up Taco and choosing the app's colours.

Make it theirs

Six colours, their pick

In My colours, your child picks a background theme for their screens — Sky, Mint, Peach, Lavender, Lemon or Blossom — and the whole app follows, from the home screen to Taco's Corner. Each child in the family gets their own choice, so siblings' apps genuinely feel like their own.

Small thing? Maybe. But children practise more in a place that feels like theirs — and it costs no XP, no money, and no nagging.

Kind, clear feedback

After the quiz: no red pens

Results screens celebrate effort ("Nice work, Casey! 3/5 correct · +31 XP") and every wrong answer is a "Keep Practising", never a failure. The review shows their answer beside the correct one — with pictures for visual questions — plus a plain-English explanation, the key skill, and the common trap.

Mistakes are quietly saved for later: the Mistakes practice mode rebuilds confidence on exactly those questions when they're ready. That loop — try, miss, understand, beat it — is the whole point of the app.
